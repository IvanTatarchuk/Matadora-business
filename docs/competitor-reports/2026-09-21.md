# Competitor Research: ConTech / Construction-Management SaaS in Poland & CEE

**Date:** 2026-09-21
**Author:** Automated research (Claude Code)
**Purpose:** Follow-up survey of the competitive landscape for construction-management SaaS in Poland and neighboring EU markets, benchmarked against Matadora's core modules (VAT-aware offer/estimate builder, investor↔contractor↔wholesaler marketplace, KSeF e-invoicing, multi-role dashboards). This is a follow-up to [2026-07-06](./2026-07-06.md), [2026-07-13](./2026-07-13.md), [2026-07-27](./2026-07-27.md), and [2026-09-14](./2026-09-14.md) — it refines the KSeF split-payment timeline further, flags a secondary source giving an incorrect simplified-invoice deadline, and adds a brief look at the Ukrainian tender landscape given Matadora's UA i18n support.

Research was performed via web search; where a source could not be directly verified or sources conflicted, it is flagged inline as unverified/uncertain rather than stated as fact.

## 1. KSeF split-payment number: confirm the field is not yet *technically* mandatory in transfer messages, only in practice from Aug 1, 2026

The 2026-09-14 report established that the KSeF number becomes a required field on split-payment (MPP) transfers "from August 1, 2026." This cycle's research adds a nuance worth flagging for `src/lib/ksef/` and the finance/payments flow: several current sources (ifirma.pl, PIT.pl, poradnikprzedsiebiorcy.pl) describe August 1, 2026 as the date the obligation to *include* the KSeF number in the MPP transfer's "invoice number" field takes effect, but simultaneously note that **technical linking/validation of split-payment transfers against the KSeF invoice number is planned to happen from January 1, 2027** — i.e., the obligation to populate the field starts in August 2026, but system-level enforcement/matching doesn't arrive until 2027.

**Why this matters:** if Matadora's payments UI already stamps the KSeF number on split-payment transfer instructions (as the 2026-09-14 report recommended verifying), that's correct and should stay as-is — populating the field early is compliant regardless of when enforcement starts. The distinction matters mainly for support/compliance messaging: don't imply to users that a missing or malformed KSeF number will bounce a split-payment transfer before 2027, since no source found in this pass or prior passes indicates bank-side or system-side validation exists yet in 2026.

Sources: [ifirma.pl — KSeF a split payment](https://www.ifirma.pl/blog/ksef-a-split-payment-jak-krajowy-system-e-faktur-wplywa-na-mechanizm-podzielonej-platnosci/), [PIT.pl — stosowanie MPP w 2026](https://www.pit.pl/aktualnosci/stosowanie-mechanizmu-podzielonej-platnosci-w-2026-r-1011868), [poradnikprzedsiebiorcy.pl — numer KSeF w płatnościach](https://poradnikprzedsiebiorcy.pl/-numer-ksef-w-platnosciach-czy-jest-obowiazek-go-podawania)

## 2. Flag: a secondary source states a "September 30, 2026" simplified-invoice deadline that the official KSeF site does not corroborate

One search result (truecommerce.com, a compliance-vendor blog) claimed small businesses issuing simplified invoices (receipts with buyer NIP, up to 450 PLN, or total monthly sales under 10,000 PLN) "may continue using paper or other electronic invoices until September 30, 2026." This is **not corroborated** by the official government KSeF portal (ksef.podatki.gov.pl) or by PIT.pl, both of which state the small-taxpayer/low-value exemption runs through **December 31, 2026**, with full structured invoicing (including simplified invoices) becoming mandatory for everyone **January 1, 2027** — consistent with every prior report in this series.

**Why this matters:** this looks like the same kind of misreading flagged in the 2026-09-14 report's Section 1 (a vendor blog conflating a partial/phase-specific date with a general deadline). No change to Matadora's own KSeF timeline understanding is warranted — the December 31, 2026 / January 1, 2027 cutover already tracked in prior reports remains correct — but it's worth noting as a reminder to treat single-source vendor blogs as lower-confidence than the official portal or multiple independent tax-advisory sites when this series cites compliance dates.

Sources: [ksef.podatki.gov.pl — Poniżej 10 000 zł](https://ksef.podatki.gov.pl/ponizej-10-000-zl/), [PIT.pl — KSeF od 2026 roku](https://www.pit.pl/aktualnosci/ksef-od-2026-roku-wyjasniamy-co-cyfrowe-faktury-oznaczaja-dla-biznesu), [truecommerce.com — the outlier claim, treated as unverified](https://www.truecommerce.com/blog/e-invoicing-in-poland-your-guide-to-the-ksef-mandate/)

## 3. New this cycle: Ukrainian construction-tender landscape is public-procurement/lead-gen only, same shape as Poland's

Given Matadora's Ukrainian i18n support, this cycle checked for a Ukrainian equivalent of Matadora's investor↔contractor↔wholesaler marketplace. None was found. The Ukrainian construction-tender space consists entirely of:

- **Prozorro-based aggregators** (e-tender.ua, SmartTender, Tender.uub.com.ua) — surface public-sector construction tenders (CPV-style categories) from Ukraine's official Prozorro public-procurement system. Discovery-only, no bidding-to-invoice workflow, no VAT/estimate tooling.
- **Rabotniki.ua** — a private-sector, consumer-facing lead-gen site for renovation/repair work with published price lists ("розцінки"), connecting individuals to contractors directly ("без посередників" — without intermediaries). Closer in spirit to a classifieds/lead-gen board than a project-management or marketplace-bidding SaaS; no estimate builder, staged VAT, or e-invoicing integration found.

This mirrors the Polish public-tender landscape already documented in the 2026-07-27 and 2026-09-14 reports (Eurobudowa, Atlas Przetargów, Oferent.com.pl, etc.) — discovery/procurement platforms exist in both markets, but nothing found in either market combines bidding, staged VAT estimating, and structured e-invoicing the way Matadora does. This is a new data point, not a new conclusion: it extends the "no direct competitor found" finding to the Ukrainian side of Matadora's addressable market as well, for whatever weight that carries given Matadora is a Poland-first product with UA localization rather than a UA-market product.

Sources: [e-tender.ua — budivelni tenderi](https://e-tender.ua/prozorro/budivelni-tenderi-16), [Rabotniki.ua — tenders](https://www.rabotniki.ua/uk/tenders), [Rabotniki.ua — розцінки 2026](https://www.rabotniki.ua/uk/price)

## 4. Re-confirmed findings

- **International pricing unchanged.** Buildertrend's flat-tier pricing (~$499-$1,099/month, unlimited users) and Procore's custom per-seat/per-module pricing (roughly $20,000-$100,000+/year depending on modules and volume) are consistent with the 2026-07-27 report. No PlanRadar or Fieldwire pricing changes found this cycle.
- **PlanRadar's Poland/CEE presence is confirmed but not new** — it entered Poland and the wider CEE region in 2020 off the back of a €30M Series A, well before this report series began. No 2026-specific expansion or product news found.
- **SimplyBud, budow.app, and BudoApka are all confirmed still active with no material feature or pricing changes** since the 2026-07-13/07-27/09-14 reports (SimplyBud: 5,000+ PLN implementation fee plus monthly subscription; budow.app: construction diary, cost estimation, schedule, KSeF integration; BudoApka: flat 139 PLN/month).
- **No new Poland-specific funding rounds found** in the construction-SaaS vertical specifically this cycle. General Polish SaaS investment capacity (e.g., Stelo Ventures Fund I, a new PFR-backed Warsaw seed fund targeting product-ready startups at PLN 3-5M per company) exists but is not construction-sector-specific and represents general market context rather than a competitor or a confirmed funding source for this space.
- The three-sided marketplace + staged VAT estimate + KSeF combination remains, across five research cycles now, the one finding no competitor search (Polish, international, or now Ukrainian) has contradicted.

## 5. Suggestions

1. **Update any internal compliance documentation or customer-facing messaging to state the split-payment KSeF-number requirement precisely: the field must be populated from August 1, 2026, but there's no evidence of system-level validation/enforcement before January 1, 2027.** This avoids over-promising ("your transfer will be rejected if malformed") or under-promising ("it's optional until 2027") — both would misstate the actual state of the rule as best understood from this and the prior report's sources.

2. **Treat single-source compliance-vendor blogs as lower-confidence than the official KSeF portal or multi-source agreement, per Section 2 above.** If any Matadora documentation, in-app help text, or `src/lib/ksef/` code comments were ever sourced from a single vendor blog rather than ksef.podatki.gov.pl or cross-checked tax-advisory sites, it's worth a pass to confirm dates against the official portal directly, given this is the second cycle in a row where a vendor blog was found to state an inaccurate or imprecise deadline.

3. **The five-cycle-consistent "no direct competitor" finding for the marketplace+VAT-estimate+KSeF combination is now strong enough to treat as validated positioning rather than a hypothesis to keep re-testing every week.** Future research cycles in this series may get more value from shifting cadence/focus — e.g., quarterly broad sweeps plus ad-hoc checks triggered by specific KSeF regulatory changes or Polish ConTech funding announcements — rather than repeating the same competitor list search-by-search with diminishing new findings, as this cycle's results (largely re-confirmations) illustrate.

## Verification caveats

The KSeF split-payment technical-enforcement-vs-field-population distinction in Section 1 is based on three independent Polish tax-advisory sources that agree with each other (ifirma.pl, PIT.pl, poradnikprzedsiebiorcy.pl), and is treated as reasonably solid, but — as with every prior report in this series — should not be treated as legally or financially authoritative without independent confirmation, especially before it's used in customer-facing compliance messaging. The Section 2 "September 30, 2026" claim is explicitly flagged as unverified/likely incorrect rather than adopted; it is included only to document that it was checked and rejected, following this series' practice of correcting rather than silently dropping conflicting claims. The Ukrainian tender-landscape findings in Section 3 are based on search-result summaries rather than direct site fetches for every listed platform, so treat feature-completeness claims (e.g., "no VAT/estimate tooling on Rabotniki.ua") as reasonably likely but not exhaustively verified.
