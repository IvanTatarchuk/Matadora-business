# Competitor Research: ConTech / Construction-Management SaaS in Poland & CEE

**Date:** 2026-11-16
**Author:** Automated research (Claude Code)
**Purpose:** Follow-up survey of the competitive landscape for construction-management SaaS in Poland and neighboring EU markets, benchmarked against Matadora's core modules (VAT-aware offer/estimate builder at 8%/23%, investor↔contractor↔wholesaler marketplace, KSeF e-invoicing, multi-role dashboards). This is a follow-up to [2026-07-06](./2026-07-06.md), [2026-07-13](./2026-07-13.md), [2026-07-27](./2026-07-27.md), [2026-09-14](./2026-09-14.md), [2026-09-21](./2026-09-21.md), [2026-09-28](./2026-09-28.md), and [2026-10-05](./2026-10-05.md). This cycle surfaced three Polish players not previously tracked in this series (Minerva, Arctis AI, MaxMaster), found the KSeF micro-enterprise timeline is now *more* contested across sources than it was a month ago rather than settling, and left KBPRO unverified for a third consecutive cycle.

Research was performed via web search only; no direct site fetches were attempted this cycle.

## 1. Three Polish players not previously tracked in this series

None of Minerva, Arctis AI, or MaxMaster appeared in the 09-28 or 10-05 reports' "Polish all-in-one players" list (ConWize, SmarteBuild, Athenasoft/Norma, budow.app, SimplyBud, BudoApka). All three came up in a general "Polish ConTech" search this cycle, which the prior reports' narrower queries apparently missed.

- **Minerva** — an AI platform for analyzing public construction tenders. Vendor-sourced copy claims its users have won over 1 billion PLN in contracts cumulatively, and cites a stat that 75–80% of construction firms never bid on a public tender at all. This is a tender-discovery/bid-fit tool, not a marketplace or execution platform — closer to a feeder into Matadora's bidding flow than a direct competitor, but worth watching since it's targeting exactly the "contractors who don't bid enough" gap that a marketplace like Matadora's is also trying to solve from the demand side.
- **Arctis AI** — an early-stage (reportedly ~$1M raised) startup building an AI agent-based contract management platform spanning tender → negotiation → execution. Described as still in pilot phase. If it matures, this is the closest thing found so far to an AI-native version of Matadora's offer→project workflow, but it's pre-market and unverified beyond funding-announcement coverage.
- **MaxMaster** — a SaaS platform for general contractors covering tendering, cost estimation (with a KNR-based costing module — the same Polish construction-norm catalog Matadora's `offer-calc.ts` stage model maps to), design, execution, and financial settlement. Of the three, this is the most direct structural overlap with Matadora's bid-to-finance scope, but all detail comes from a single portfolio/freelance-work listing (Freelancehunt), not the vendor's own site or an independent review — treat as low-confidence until verified directly.

**Relevance to Matadora:** none of these three were found advertising a multi-role (investor/contractor/wholesaler) marketplace structure — they're single-sided tools (tender analytics, contract AI, GC back-office) rather than three-sided marketplaces. That keeps Matadora's three-role model distinctive for now, but MaxMaster's KNR-based tender→finance scope is close enough to Matadora's own that it deserves a direct look (pricing, actual customer base) in a future cycle rather than resting on a single secondhand source.

## 2. KSeF: the micro-enterprise timeline got *less* clear this cycle, not more

The 10-05 report flagged a discrepancy between an Oct 1, 2026 and a Jan 1, 2027 date for small/"digitally excluded" taxpayers and suggested verifying against the official portal. This cycle's search didn't resolve that — it added more disagreement:

- Multiple sources now converge on **Feb 1, 2026** (large taxpayers) and **Apr 1, 2026** (everyone else) as fixed and already in the past — those two dates are no longer in dispute anywhere in this series.
- For **micro-enterprises** (sources specify a monthly-turnover test, under 10,000 PLN/month, checked per month rather than annually), **Jan 1, 2027** is the most commonly cited mandatory start date this cycle — consistent with the 09-28 report's figure, not the 10-05 report's "Oct 1, 2026" alternative. That earlier date did not reappear in this cycle's results, which is weak evidence it may have been a misread of a different carve-out rather than a real second deadline — but this report can't confirm that either way.
- The **exemption window** before that mandatory date is itself disputed across sources found this cycle: one gives **Apr 1 – Sep 30, 2026**, another gives **Feb 1 – Dec 31, 2026**. These can't both be right as stated.
- **Penalties** are now the most confused item in the whole KSeF thread across this series: one source repeats the 10-05 report's "no fines until Jan 1, 2027" position; another says the Ministry has signaled a lenient approach to *technical* errors specifically through 2026 (implying non-technical errors may not get the same grace); a third claims "jedna kara za KSeF obowiązuje już teraz" (one specific penalty is already in force) without this search surfacing which penalty that refers to.

**Why this matters:** this is now the second consecutive cycle where KSeF deadline/penalty details conflict across sources rather than converging, and the newest conflict (penalties possibly already partial-in-force) is more consequential for customer-facing messaging than the small-business date question was. The 10-05 report's suggestion to "proactively communicate the Jan 1, 2027 penalty-grace detail to users" should not go out verbatim until this is checked against the official KSeF portal or a tax advisory source — stating "no penalties until 2027" could now be wrong.

Sources: [jpk.info.pl — Harmonogram wdrożenia obowiązkowych e-faktur 2026–2027](https://jpk.info.pl/faktury/e-faktura-ustrukturyzowana-ksef/harmonogram/), [jpk.info.pl — KSeF: dla kogo jest obowiązkowy](https://jpk.info.pl/faktury/e-faktura-ustrukturyzowana-ksef/dla-kogo/), [bank.pl — Już wiadomo jakie problemy będą mieli mikroprzedsiębiorcy z KSeF](https://bank.pl/juz-wiadomo-jakie-problemy-beda-mieli-mikroprzedsiebiorcy-z-ksef/), [ifirma.pl — Wydłużenie okresu ochronnego przy wdrażaniu KSeF](https://www.ifirma.pl/blog/wydluzenie-okresu-ochronnego-przy-wdrazaniu-krajowego-systemu-e-faktur/), [bezprawnik.pl — Jedna kara za KSeF obowiązuje już teraz](https://bezprawnik.pl/jedna-kara-za-ksef-obowiazuje-juz-teraz-i-grozi-nawet-kilkaset-tysiecy-zlotych/)

## 4. Re-confirmed findings (no material change from 10-05)

- **International incumbents** — Procore (quote-based, roughly $4.5K–$150K+/yr depending on source, commercial GC/owner focus), Buildertrend (pricing no longer published on its own site per 2026 sources; third-party estimates place tiers from ~$99–$339/mo up to $800–$1,100/mo, residential/remodeler focus), Fieldwire (free tier up to 5 users/3 projects/100 sheets, paid tier cited around $39/user/mo, field coordination only — no financial module, owned by Hilti), PlanRadar (Vienna-based, three published tiers around €26 / €89 / €129 per user/month — figures consistent with the 10-05 report, active in CEE with a Warsaw presence) — all substantively unchanged. None of these publish PLN-native pricing or a public/KSeF-aware VAT billing path, and none combine estimating + marketplace bidding + invoicing + multi-role dashboards the way Matadora does; they each cover one or two of those layers at most.
- **Polish all-in-one estimating players** — ConWize (cloud estimating/bid-management, integrates with Autodesk Build/Docs, Procore, Priority Software; mid-size GC focus; pricing still quote-only) and Athenasoft/Norma (desktop KNR-based estimating suite, recently added a new KNR catalog for road-surface work and an "AthGOZ" sustainability tool to its AthGreen platform) — no material change in competitive posture; still estimating-only tools, not marketplaces.
- **Oferteo** — still a reverse-marketplace lead-generation funnel (client posts a request, multiple contractors quote), spanning many service categories beyond construction; not a bid-to-invoice workflow competitor.
- **Public-tender/procurement aggregators** (Eurobudowa, Oferent.com.pl, Biznes-Polska, Kompas Inwestycji, Platforma Zakupowa) and **Grupa PSB** (materials distribution network, no contractor bidding marketplace) — unchanged in classification.
- **AI-assisted estimating as an emerging baseline** (flagged new in 10-05) — no additional evidence found this cycle either confirming or denying that a Polish competitor has shipped this; the gap identified in 10-05 appears to still be open.

## 5. Suggestions

1. **Hold off on any customer-facing "no KSeF penalties until Jan 1, 2027" messaging** until it's checked against the official KSeF portal or a tax advisory source. This cycle surfaced a source claiming a specific penalty is already in force, which contradicts the clean "grace period through 2026" framing the 10-05 report suggested communicating to users. Getting this wrong in a user-facing compliance message is a real support/legal-exposure risk, more so than any competitive-positioning item in this report.

2. **Take a direct, verified look at MaxMaster** before relying on this report's characterization of it. It's the closest structural analog to Matadora found in this series so far (tender → KNR-based costing → execution → finance, for general contractors), but everything known about it currently traces to a single secondhand portfolio listing. If it turns out to be a real, live product with Polish-market traction, it's a more material competitive signal than anything else in this report; if it's a shelved freelance project, it's not worth tracking further.

3. **Stop re-running the KBPRO web search and either fetch it directly or drop it.** Three consecutive cycles (09-28, 10-05, and this one) have produced zero new information on kbpro.com.pl through this research environment's search-only access. The next cycle should either attempt a direct fetch from an unblocked environment/browser, or explicitly mark KBPRO as "unverified, deprioritized" in the next report rather than repeating the same unresolved flag a fourth time.

## Verification caveats

The KSeF micro-enterprise exemption window (Apr–Sep 2026 vs. Feb–Dec 2026) and the penalty-timing claims in Section 2 are reported inconsistently across sources found this cycle and are *more* contested than in the 10-05 report, not less — none of this should be treated as settled without checking the official KSeF portal (ksef.podatki.gov.pl) or a tax advisor directly. Minerva, Arctis AI, and MaxMaster (Section 1) are each sourced from a single vendor-adjacent or secondhand listing rather than independent verification, and should be treated as low-confidence leads for a future cycle, not confirmed competitive facts. KBPRO (Section 3) remains entirely unverified after three cycles. International-incumbent pricing (Section 4) is consistent with the 10-05 report but, as in every prior report in this series, comes from third-party/affiliate comparison sites rather than official vendor pricing pages and should be confirmed directly before being used in any pricing or positioning decision. All caveats from the [2026-07-06](./2026-07-06.md) through [2026-10-05](./2026-10-05.md) reports still apply.
