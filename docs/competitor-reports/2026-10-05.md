## 1. KSeF: the mandate is no longer a future date — most of Matadora's users are now actually subject to it

Every prior report in this series tracked KSeF as an approaching deadline. As of this report's date (2026-10-05), that's no longer true for most businesses:

- **Feb 1, 2026** (large taxpayers, >200M PLN turnover) and **Apr 1, 2026** (most other VAT-registered businesses) have both already passed — KSeF use is mandatory for the large majority of Matadora's contractor and wholesaler users right now, not "coming soon."
- **New this cycle:** sources diverge slightly on the small-business/"digitally excluded" taxpayer timeline. One source states mandatory KSeF use for small-scale taxpayers issuing a limited number of invoices began **Oct 1, 2026** (i.e., days ago); the previously-tracked date in the 09-28 report was **Jan 1, 2027** for micro-taxpayers. This may reflect two different taxpayer categories (a narrow "digitally excluded" carve-out vs. the general micro-business deadline) rather than a contradiction, but it should be verified directly against the official KSeF portal rather than assumed — the practical effect for Matadora either way is that essentially all of its wholesaler and contractor user base is now inside the mandate or about to be within months.
- **Penalty timing is the one piece of slack left:** administrative financial penalties under the VAT Act apply only from **Jan 1, 2027**, and an **offline mode has been extended to all taxpayers through the end of 2026** in response to industry feedback. So the system is live and mandatory, but enforcement has a grace period — this is the detail worth putting in front of users directly ("you're required to use it now, but won't be fined until next year, and offline fallback is still allowed").
- The **FA(3) structured-invoice format** and the **KSeF 2.0 API** (open for testing since Sept 30, 2025) remain the technical target tracked since the 09-28 report; no new information this cycle on whether that generation has fully superseded the 1.0 API in production.

**Why this matters more than it did two weeks ago:** this stops being a "track the deadline" item and becomes a "is `src/lib/ksef/` actually live and handling real invoice volume correctly" item. If Matadora's KSeF module still targets a pre-2.0 API surface, the gap between "integration exists" and "integration is correct against the live FA(3) production system" is now a real support/compliance risk for active customers, not a forward-looking roadmap concern.

Sources: [ksef.podatki.gov.pl — Etapy wdrożenia KSeF 2.0](https://ksef.podatki.gov.pl/etapy-wdrozenia-ksef/), [bank.pl — Obowiązkowy KSeF dla wszystkich przedsiębiorców od kwietnia 2026 roku](https://bank.pl/obowiazkowy-ksef-dla-wszystkich-przedsiebiorcow-od-kwietnia-2026-roku/), [Crowe — KSeF penalties and how to avoid them](https://www.crowe.com/pl/en-us/insights/ksef-what-penalties-and-how-avoid-them), [MDDP — KSeF 2.0 takes shape](https://www.mddp.pl/ksef-2-0-takes-shape-what-changes-in-the-legislation/)

## 2. New industry trend: AI-assisted estimating and bid prep is becoming a baseline feature, not a differentiator

This wasn't a theme in prior cycles' searches but showed up clearly this time:

- Cost estimation and bid management are now cited as the **top two AI use cases** among contractors (roughly 24% and 22% of surveyed AI adoption respectively), and AI-powered estimating tools are described as cutting bid-prep time by ~40% with 85–90% accuracy on automated quantity takeoffs from plans.
- Vendors like Trimble (ProjectSight AI) are shipping ML-based takeoff/line-item recognition directly from drawings; the framing across multiple sources is that AI estimating has moved from "experimental nice-to-have" to "expected baseline" for 2026.
- None of the Poland-specific players tracked in this series (ConWize, SmarteBuild, budow.app, SimplyBud, BudoApka) were found to advertise AI-assisted estimating in this search round — so the gap, where it exists, is between Polish-market players and the US/global incumbents, not something a Polish competitor has already closed.

**Relevance to Matadora:** `computeOfferTotals()` and the stage-based estimate model (Logistics/Demolition/Material/Labor, VAT-staged) are exactly the kind of structured line-item data that an AI-assisted "suggest stages/quantities from a short project description" feature could sit on top of, without needing drawing-takeoff capability (which is a much bigger lift). This looks like a differentiation opportunity specifically *because* no Polish competitor found so far has it, while international incumbents are normalizing it as a baseline expectation.

Sources: [Beck Technology — Estimating in 2026](https://www.beck-technology.com/blog/estimating-in-2026-economic-shifts-labor-shortages-and-the-ai-transformation-of-preconstruction), [AnalyticsInsight — AI in Construction Estimating 2026](https://www.analyticsinsight.net/amp/story/artificial-intelligence/ai-in-construction-estimating-a-complete-guide-for-2026), [ConstructionPlacements — Best AI Estimating Software](https://www.constructionplacements.com/best-ai-estimating-software-construction/)

## 5. Suggestions

1. **Treat the KSeF module as a live-production compliance surface now, not a roadmap item.** With the Feb/Apr 2026 mandatory dates already passed and a possible Oct 2026 extension to small-scale taxpayers, confirm `src/lib/ksef/` is actually validated against the FA(3)/KSeF 2.0 API (open for testing since Sept 30, 2025) and supports the offline mode that's been extended through end of 2026 — and proactively communicate the Jan 1, 2027 penalty-grace detail to users, since "mandatory but not yet fined" is a nuance customers are likely to get wrong on their own.

2. **Consider a scoped AI-assisted estimating feature as a near-term differentiator**, before Polish competitors close the gap international incumbents are opening. Given the existing stage-based estimate structure (Logistics/Demolition/Material/Labor with VAT staging), a "suggest stages and typical line items from a short project description" assistant is a much smaller lift than drawing-based takeoff AI and would put Matadora ahead of every Polish-market competitor tracked in this series, at least for now.

3. **Close out the KBPRO verification instead of re-flagging it a third time.** Two consecutive cycles have hit the same network block; if this research environment can't reach kbpro.com.pl, the next cycle should either route this specific check through a different access path (a teammate's browser, a cached/archived copy of the site) or formally deprioritize it until someone can look directly, rather than repeating an unverifiable search each week.
